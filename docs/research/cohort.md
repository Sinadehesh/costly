# Cohort — cold-approach user research

Method: strangers approached cold and screened on two questions — *how much do
you use Instagram?* and *do you want to cut back?* — before being shown
anything. Those who qualified walked through onboarding on a phone while
thinking aloud.

**n = 9.** Convenience sample, self-moderated, no control. It generates
hypotheses; it does not prove anything. Say that out loud before quoting it.

> ⚠️ **Numbering to reconcile.** An earlier session was reported as "5 — cinema
> student, 28, self-described 'super ADHD', heavy cannabis use, didn't know her
> own usage, chose €15/hr and the maximum €1000 fee, and asked where she could
> download the app." No such participant appears in the current 1–9 list.
> Either she was dropped or the numbering shifted. Resolve before citing counts
> — her session contained the strongest behavioural signal recorded (an
> unprompted request for the real product) and it should not be lost.

## Findings

### 1. The screening answer predicts conversion better than usage does

Everyone who converted had already lost an argument with themselves:

- #1 — *"I'm very harsh with myself and I really want to do it"*
- #7 — *"when this thing is in my hand I cannot control myself"*

Everyone who declined believes they still hold the wheel:

- #6 — *"if I really need to stop using Insta I can stop myself, I did it before"*
- #9 — *"I'd rather control myself, even if I fail"*

Note #8: only **30 minutes a day** and still positive, because he already runs
an Apple limit. Heavy usage is not the qualifier — **a history of failed
self-control is.** That is the segment, and it is a sharper targeting rule than
any usage threshold.

### 2. Comprehension failed three times out of nine

#5 didn't understand the product until asked to read the text. #9 needed
"a couple of minutes" to get it. #4 misread the mechanism entirely. For a
product that charges a card automatically, that is the most expensive defect on
the list.

**Shipped:** the mechanic is now a gated first step, revealed one tap at a time,
ending in a red panel and a required acknowledgement.

### 3. Nobody must be able to mistake this for play money

#6, unprompted and emphatically:

> *"some people could mistake it and download it and think this money is not
> real money and get fucked — so you need to be very careful, and make it very
> clear that it's your real money and your credit card and you will be charged
> on it."*

**Shipped:** the explainer's closing panel, verbatim in spirit — *"Real money.
Your real credit card. Costly charges it automatically, without asking again."*

### 4. The time-cost figure is the most-loved thing in the product

Two independent participants, unprompted:

- #8 — *"he saw the hours in years… that's quite a good indicator, you should put
  it in red"*
- #9 — checked **every** free-minute option just to compare the hours:
  *"I appreciate how much it is in the whole year, it teaches me so much, I like
  it"* — then asked for it as a forecast: *"on this rate you will waste 50 hours
  this year, or 2 years of your life"*

**Shipped:** current usage is now the first question in step 2, and it renders
as days-per-year and years-per-decade in red. Money is deliberately the
footnote — she reacted to the hours, not the euros — and it is framed as what
the time is *worth* at her rate, never as a bill we intend to send, because
caps and the free allowance mean those are different numbers.

### 5. The free allowance is a feature, not a concession

#7 — *"I really like that you allow this, this is very good."* #2 asked for it.
#8 set 15 minutes to match his existing Apple limit. The earlier copy argued
hard *against* using it; that was the wrong posture and has been cut to three
lines that name the excuse instead of scolding it.

### 6. Two distinct reasons for "no", and only one is fixable

- **Affordability** (#3, #5): prepaid cards, no income, real fear of loss. Not a
  copy problem. Either a very low starter stake, a no-card practice week, or
  honest exclusion.
- **Mechanism mismatch** (#4, #9): #4 scrolls to regulate mood — *"I need
  doomscrolling to chill… so even if I have it I don't necessarily decrease"* —
  and #9 won't be monitored at all. Price does not touch either. These are
  non-responders, and knowing that is worth more than converting them.

## The gap in this data

Only the demo funnel was measured. Not measured: how many people were
approached to find 9 qualifiers, and whether anyone would pay **after** a real
charge landed. The first is one tally mark per approach and should start
immediately; the second is what the alpha is for.
